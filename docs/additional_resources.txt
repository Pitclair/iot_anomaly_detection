
The closest recent literature falls into three areas:
  adaptive/online IoT anomaly detection, unsupervised statistical detection, and lightweight/explainable edge deployment.

  One important finding is that very little post-2022 research applies the Dirichlet–Multinomial directly to IoT network anomaly detection. That strengthens the
  project’s claimed research gap.

  ## Most closely related literature

  1. Self-Adaptive Traffic Anomaly Detection System for IoT Smart Home Environments — Watanabe et al., 2025, IEICE Transactions on Communications.

     Proposes a gateway-based detector whose model is continuously regenerated from honeypot traffic. This is probably the closest external paper to LM-IDNet’s
     adaptive architecture and unknown-attack objective. Its use of potentially malicious traffic for adaptation also makes it useful when discussing
     model-poisoning safeguards. Paper and DOI (https://doi.org/10.23919/transcom.2024EBT0002)

  2. Online Self-Supervised Deep Learning for Intrusion Detection Systems — Nakıp and Gelenbe, 2024, IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, 19,
     5668–5683.

     Presents a fully online, self-supervised IDS that does not require prior offline training. It is an excellent comparison for LM-IDNet’s online estimation and
     adaptive retraining, although it uses a deep random neural network rather than a lightweight count model. IEEE record
     (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10531267) · DOI (https://doi.org/10.1109/TIFS.2024.3402148)

  3. A Contaminated Model for Overdispersed Multinomial Microbiome Count Data — van Heerden et al., 2026, preprint.

     The most directly relevant recent statistical paper. It introduces a two-component contaminated Dirichlet–Multinomial model in which ordinary observations and
     anomalies have different dispersion levels, and anomaly probabilities emerge from posterior inference. Although the application is microbiome data rather than
     IoT traffic, the method could provide a strong theoretical extension or baseline for LM-IDNet. Preprint (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02199)

  4. Intrusion Detection in the IoT Data Streams Using Concept Drift Localization — Chu et al., 2024, AIMS Mathematics, 9(1).

     Introduces an ensemble of non-parametric concept-localization detectors for non-stationary IoT streams. It directly supports the thesis sections on drift
     detection, safe retraining, and distinguishing behavioral evolution from security anomalies. Open-access paper (https://doi.org/10.3934/math.2024076)

  5. CAEAID: An Incremental Contrast Learning-Based Intrusion Detection Framework for IoT Networks — Yin et al., 2025, Computer Networks, 262, 111161.

     Combines incremental contrastive learning, adaptive anomaly detection, and model updating under concept drift. This is a strong modern adaptive baseline,
     especially when comparing LM-IDNet’s statistical simplicity against deep incremental learning. Publisher page (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comnet.2025.111161)

  6. CADeSH: Collaborative Anomaly Detection for Smart Homes — Meidan et al., 2023 version, IEEE Internet of Things Journal.

     Learns normal behavior from multiple identical consumer IoT devices using an autoencoder and clustering. It reports low false-positive rates using real
     traffic collected from devices in different homes and discusses generalization, privacy, resource savings, and poisoning mitigation—all highly relevant to
     LM-IDNet. IEEE record (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9844137) · Open preprint (https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01021)

  7. Robust Unsupervised Network Intrusion Detection with Self-Supervised Masked Context Reconstruction — 2023, Computers & Security, 128, 103131.

     Introduces RUIDS, a transformer-based unsupervised detector designed to tolerate contaminated training data. It is particularly relevant to the thesis’s
     requirement that anomalous windows must not be absorbed into the normal profile. Publisher page (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2023.103131)

  8. Histogram-Based Network Traffic Representation for Anomaly Detection Through PCA — Baldoni and Battisti, 2025, Computer Networks, 265, 111276.

     Uses compact histogram summaries of traffic windows with a completely unsupervised, low-complexity PCA detector. Because LM-IDNet also converts traffic
     windows into compact count vectors, this is an especially appropriate non-neural baseline for both accuracy and computational-cost comparisons. Open-access
     paper (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comnet.2025.111276)

  9. Supervision and Early Warning of Abnormal Data in Internet of Things Based on Unsupervised Attention Learning — 2024, Computer Communications.

     Combines edge-side unsupervised anomaly detection with a Dirichlet-process mixture model. This is not a Dirichlet–Multinomial model, but it is one of the
     closer recent works combining Bayesian non-parametric modeling, unsupervised IoT anomaly detection, edge execution, and early warning. Publisher page
     (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comcom.2023.11.019)

  10. XAI-IoT: An Explainable AI Framework for Enhancing Anomaly Detection in IoT Systems — Gummadi, Napier, and Abdallah, 2024, IEEE Access, 12, 71024–71054.

     Provides an explainable anomaly-detection framework for IoT sensor and network data. It is directly relevant to LM-IDNet’s proposed per-category explanations
     and would help structure evaluation of explanation quality rather than reporting only detection metrics. IEEE/DOI page
     (https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3402446)

  11. Network Traffic Inspection to Enhance Anomaly Detection in the Internet of Things Using Attention-Driven Deep Learning — 2025, Integration, 103, 102398.

     Learns network-traffic representations from nine generic flow and protocol features, then performs unsupervised detection with a one-class SVM. Protocol
     presence is particularly close to LM-IDNet’s categorical protocol-count features. Publisher page (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vlsi.2025.102398)

  12. FedMSE: Semi-Supervised Federated Learning Approach for IoT Network Intrusion Detection — Nguyen and Beuran, 2025, Computers & Security, 151, 104337.

     Uses normal-data representation, a one-class classifier, and federated aggregation for heterogeneous IoT gateways. It is relevant to future multi-device or
     hierarchical LM-IDNet extensions and to privacy-preserving edge deployment. Publisher page (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2025.104337) · Open preprint
     (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14121)

  13. Cross-Layer Federated Learning for Lightweight IoT Intrusion Detection Systems — Hajj et al., 2023, Sensors, 23(16), 7038.

     Examines lightweight detection distributed across IoT layers. It is useful for the edge-deployment chapter and for comparing centralized per-device
     Dirichlet–Multinomial profiles with collaborative or federated alternatives. Open-access paper (https://doi.org/10.3390/s23167038)

  14. Quantifying and Managing Impacts of Concept Drifts on IoT Traffic Inference in Residential ISP Networks — Pashamokhtari et al., 2023, preprint.

     Studies more than six million flow records from 24 consumer-IoT device types across 12 real homes. It demonstrates temporal and spatial drift in device
     traffic and evaluates model-selection strategies for unseen homes. This is strong empirical justification for LM-IDNet’s adaptive and device-specific modeling
     decisions. Preprint (https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06695)

  15. Anomaly Detection in Extremes — Trubey and Sansó, 2023, UCSC Technical Report.

     Develops likelihood-based anomaly scores and uses a Dirichlet–Multinomial kernel for categorical data. It is not IoT-specific, but it is directly relevant to
     probabilistic anomaly scoring for categorical observations and could inform more theoretically grounded thresholds than empirical quantiles alone. Report page
     (https://tr.soe.ucsc.edu/research/technical-reports/UCSC-SOE-23-12) · PDF (https://tr.soe.ucsc.edu/sites/default/files/technical-reports/UCSC-SOE-23-12.pdf)

  ## Useful recent reviews

  Two surveys would be particularly helpful when expanding the thesis literature review:

  - On-the-Fly, Memory-Aware Unsupervised Learning for Network Anomaly Detection: A Systematic Literature Review — 2025. Reviews 121 studies and reports that fewer
    than approximately 15% evaluate genuine online adaptation. This strongly supports LM-IDNet’s research gap. Open-access article
    (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prime.2025.101146)

  - A Systematic Literature Review of Recent Lightweight Detection Approaches Leveraging Machine and Deep Learning Mechanisms in Internet of Things Networks — Al
    Mukhaini et al., 2024, Journal of King Saud University – Computer and Information Sciences, 36, 101866. Useful for selecting edge-oriented baselines and
    reporting runtime, memory, and model-size metrics. Open-access article (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jksuci.2023.101866)

  The strongest immediate additions to the thesis are papers 1–8. Paper 3 supplies the closest recent Dirichlet–Multinomial anomaly model; papers 1, 2, 4, and 5
  cover adaptation; paper 7 addresses contaminated normal training; and paper 8 gives a lightweight unsupervised baseline operating on compact traffic summaries.
