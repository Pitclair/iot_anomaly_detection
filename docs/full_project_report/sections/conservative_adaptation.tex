\section{Conservative Adaptation and Legacy Access}
\label{sec:conservative}

\subsection{Why the policies are separate}

The historical adaptation experiments showed visible changes, including a useful
Chromecast improvement. They also exposed Samsung contamination and large
parameter movement. The software now keeps those earlier methods under the
explicit \texttt{legacy} policy while making \texttt{conservative} the default.
Both policies support static, threshold-adaptive, and periodic-refit modes.
The mode chooses what may change; the policy chooses which observations and
candidates are allowed to change it.

This distinction matters when reading old results. The historical threshold
method uses all recent scored windows, including anomalies. A legacy refit uses
the original baseline-confidence gate and checks convergence and likelihood,
but it does not impose the newer volume and movement limits. A fitting
rejection may still lead to a legacy threshold fallback. The conservative
policy rejects the complete candidate pair and leaves both model and threshold
unchanged.

\subsection{Admission to the learning buffer}

Let $s_t$ be the underlying DM score under the active model and $s_0$ its score
under the original model. The default conservative gate requires
\[
s_t(\mathbf{x})\geq\tau_t+m\,\mathrm{IQR}_t,\qquad
s_0(\mathbf{x})\geq\tau_0+m\,\mathrm{IQR}_0,\qquad N\leq V.
\]
The margin $m$ defaults to one. The fixed volume limit $V$ is the 0.99 empirical
quantile of benign calibration totals. A flagged or near-threshold window is
not a high-confidence learning example; a large total is rejected even if the
protocol mixture looks ordinary. The current window is scored first, so an
update can affect only later events.

The volume limit $V$ is a learning safeguard, distinct from the jointly
calibrated volume/ARP detection cutoffs. With combined scoring, both count
margins must also clear $m$ before admission. The fixed count cutoffs and
original DM remain detection references after updates, preventing adaptation
from suppressing their evidence (Section~\ref{sec:detector-scoring}).

Accepted counts are held in a bounded deque. The usual buffer contains at most
288 windows. Counters record excessive-volume and low-confidence rejections.
Labels are absent from this decision. Original safety references are retained
after updates and across restoration rather than moving along with every new
model.

\subsection{Candidate fitting and movement limits}

Threshold-only candidates are calculated from accepted counts scored under the
active model. A periodic candidate refits alpha on accepted counts, with one
single-count smoothing row per category, then recalculates its matching
threshold and IQR. It needs enough samples for both fitting and calibration.
Parameters must be finite, positive, and dimensionally compatible; the fit
must converge and must not worsen likelihood beyond the configured tolerance.

The default movement bounds are
\[
\frac{1}{1.25}\leq
\frac{\alpha'_0}{\alpha_{0,t}}\leq1.25,\qquad
\max_k|p'_k-p_{k,t}|\leq0.05,\qquad
|\tau'-\tau_t|\leq2\,\mathrm{IQR}_0.
\]
The candidate IQR must be finite and positive. A failed conservative refit
cannot fall back to an unrelated threshold change. A valid candidate is saved
as a complete pair before it becomes active in memory.

The likelihood comparison currently uses the fitting buffer, not an independent
candidate holdout. The source records that limit. Per-update movement bounds
also do not prove a bound on cumulative movement over an unlimited sequence of
updates. Neither safeguard establishes immunity to stealthy attacks that
already resemble accepted traffic.

\subsection{What a rejected update proves}

A rejected candidate proves that its configured gate prevented activation.
It does not show useful adaptation. Successful promotion in a controlled test
proves that the code can accept a permitted update; it does not establish an
improvement on real traffic. The long Docker trial adds measured rejection cost,
not a positive adaptation result.

The cross-device comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:retained-scoring} record candidate
outcomes. At the existing settings, Chromecast and Samsung reject every
conservative candidate, leaving their decisions equal to static detection.
These results document the gates' behaviour without establishing useful
conservative learning on those traces. The six schedule-specific
configuration directories are a separate evidence bundle and remain incomplete.

\subsection{How earlier methods remain accessible}

The existing \texttt{adapt} command and Python
\path{adapt_windows(config)} entry point select policy through configuration.
The streaming runtime uses the same policy field. No HTTP service is needed
to expose the existing software methods. Six legacy configurations cover both
labelled devices and the original threshold/refit schedules, with isolated
outputs under \path{artifacts/experiments/legacy/} and
\path{reports/experiments/legacy/}.

The retained \path{reports/experiments/legacy/reproduction_check.json}
documents exact event-field, final-state, and metric agreement with the
historical implementation at its recorded boundary. Later input-contract
migration is a separate provenance change. Current tests also compare batch
and streaming behaviour across both policies, both score conventions, and all
three modes. These checks preserve access without presenting legacy risk as a
property of the conservative default.
