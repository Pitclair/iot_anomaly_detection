\section{Conclusion}

LM-IDNet now contains a complete supplied-window detector implementation and a
substantial body of research evidence. The software processes device traffic,
fits and calibrates a DM model, exposes static and adaptive methods, validates
input compatibility, saves accepted state safely, restores and rolls it back,
and runs a bounded streaming service in a measured local Docker simulation.
The native likelihood work preserves the intended statistical objective and
records both numerical agreement and workload-dependent runtime.

The evidence also explains the system's limits. Protocol composition misses
some attacks. Legacy learning can improve one development sequence while
worsening another and admitting contaminated observations. Conservative guards
prevent specified unsafe changes. Jointly calibrated volume/ARP scoring and
fixed detection references improve pooled legacy-adaptive results and Chromecast
score ranking while keeping the DM core; static and conservative decisions on
Chromecast and Samsung are unchanged.
Synthetic rejection and controlled promotion remain different evidence from
those retrospective results. Temporal research retains its historical scope in the appendix.

The 10/20/30/60-minute comparison shows why duration needs a detection objective
beyond the reference paper's precedent. Thirty minutes improves static attack
coverage, while ten minutes gives earlier alerts. One hour adds substantial
delay, and longer-window threshold adaptation can increase false alerts.
The default remains ten minutes; a move to thirty-minute static operation
requires an explicit response-time tradeoff and further validation.

The completed final-partition evaluation gives 80.00\% pooled F1 across 1,008
labelled windows, with 14 true positives, three false positives, and four missed
attack windows. Static and conservative adaptive decisions coincide because no
updates are promoted; eight of nine official attack intervals are detected.
These results complete the selected detector’s evaluation. The small number
of attack windows limits the precision of estimates across attack types
and devices.

The code, supported interfaces, tests, final evidence bundle, and full project
report complete the agreed deliverable. The retained method is the four-count
DM model with joint composition/volume/ARP scoring, fixed detection references,
conservative adaptation, and ten-minute windows. Independent external testing,
rolling-fold integration, physical gateway measurements, and dissertation
submission are defined future work. They do not change the completed scope or
turn historical unsuccessful experiments into successful results.
