\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
IoT devices can exhibit characteristic network behaviour, making learned
normal profiles a useful basis for anomaly monitoring. This thesis presents
LM-IDNet, a device-specific detector using TCP, ordinary UDP, SSDP, and ARP
counts in completed ten-minute windows. A Dirichlet--multinomial model
represents traffic composition, complemented by jointly calibrated total-volume
and ARP checks. Fitting and calibration use separate presumed-benign periods;
attack labels enter offline evaluation and method selection. The system
integrates the established Languasco--Migliardi likelihood method with
Minka estimation, guarded threshold/model updates, and persistent state.

Experiments distinguish labelled Chromecast and Samsung camera detection
from an unlabelled D-Link numerical workload. A fixed closing replay covers
1,008 unique labelled windows and yields pooled precision of 82.35\%, recall
of 77.78\%, and F1 of 80.00\%. Device F1 differs substantially, at 61.54\%
and 90.91\%. Eight of nine fully observed attack intervals are detected;
seven receive an alert by attack end. The later partition had prior label
exposure in retrospective audits, limiting independent generalisation claims.
Conservative modes accept no closing updates and match static decisions.
Historical refitting and other-duration threshold experiments show that
adaptation can reduce detection quality. Coarser windows can improve their
own window metrics while delaying alerts.

LM and SciPy preserve all checked development decisions, with close numerical
agreement and operation-dependent runtime. A one-CPU, 512~MiB Linux/amd64
Docker simulation scores 16,000 windows with mean processing near 30~ms
and process high-water memory near 69~MiB; its longest window takes about
5.08~s during a rejected refit. These measurements cover supplied-window
processing, excluding live collection and physical gateways. The thesis
provides a reproducible, interpretable prototype and identifies representation,
adaptation, and evaluation limits that require further independent study.
